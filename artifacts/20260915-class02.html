Three failure modes in finite precision

  1. Increment too small relative to the current partial sum.
  2. The term itself is smaller than the smallest positive representable number.
  3. The accumulated partial sum leaves the upper representable range.

Changing the summation order does not change the mathematical sum, but it can change the floating-point result.

Numerical diagnostic

Evaluate the same mathematical quantity using more than one reasonable association pattern: direct summation, chunks of 500, chunks of 1,000, chunks of 10,000, and so on. If the answers vary substantially, the numerical result is algorithm-dependent and should not be treated as a reliable approximation.

Why “the sum stopped changing” does not prove convergence

A genuinely convergent sequence and a finite-precision sequence whose increments have become too small can both appear constant. Detecting identifies a machine limitation, but by itself it does not distinguish mathematical convergence from numerical stagnation.

8. Main takeaways

  • Floating-point arithmetic operates on a finite set of numbers.
  • Representable range and machine precision are separate properties.
  • Absolute spacing grows with magnitude even when the relative precision is fixed.
  • Catastrophic cancellation can remove significant digits.
  • Floating-point addition is not reliably associative.
  • Algebraically equivalent formulas can have very different numerical behavior.
  • Changing the summation order is a useful stress test when the exact answer is unknown.
